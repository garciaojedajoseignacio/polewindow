    Site detail

    Method

    1. Geometry. Positions of the Sun and Earth seen from the Moon come from the NASA JPL DE421 planetary ephemeris, rotated into the lunar Mean Earth / Polar Axis frame (MOON_ME_DE421) using NAIF SPICE kernels, via the open-source Skyfield library. Light-time and aberration are included.
    2. Sampling. Every hour of 2027 (UTC), 8760 samples. For each location we compute the topocentric elevation and azimuth of the Sun's center and Earth's center.
    3. Spherical horizon. A body is "visible" if its center is above 0° elevation on a smooth sphere. This ignores mountains and craters.
    4. Terrain-aware horizon (default). From the LRO LOLA polar DEM (240 m/pixel) we cast 360 rays (1° azimuth steps) out to 150 km, sample terrain every 240 m along great circles, account for lunar curvature, and keep the highest elevation angle per azimuth. The observer is 2 m above the LOLA surface. A body is visible if its center is above the horizon at its azimuth.
    5. Metrics. Sun % (power), Earth % (direct-to-Earth radio line of sight), Sun + Earth % (both at once) and the longest continuous period without the Sun's center visible.
    6. Grid. — points: 1.5 km spacing within 36 km of the pole, 6 km spacing out to 84°S, all in the same projection as the map.

    Validation

    • Our vectorized elevation code matches Skyfield's own topocentric calculation for all five sites to —° (Jan–Mar 2027).
    • The underlying Skyfield pipeline was cross-checked against JPL Horizons (which used DE441 in that query) for Sun and Earth elevations at the sites.
    • The DEM orientation was checked against known features (Shackleton crater appears as a ~2.4 km deep depression at its gazetteer center), and the most sunlit grid points (up to about 68% of 2027) cluster on the Shackleton rim and the connecting ridge, the same areas reported as highly illuminated in published LOLA studies (e.g., Mazarico et al., 2011). Our maxima are lower than published values because of the 1.5 km grid, the 240 m DEM and the center-of-disk rule.

    Limitations

    • Visibility uses the body's center; partial solar disk, solar panel height and orientation, and Earth's partial disk are not modeled.
    • 240 m DEM resolution smooths summits and small ridges; real illumination at a specific lander position can differ. Higher-resolution LOLA products (5–20 m) exist but were not used for this demo.
    • One year (2027) only; the lunar polar illumination pattern changes over the ~18.6-year nodal cycle.
    • Earth visibility is geometric line of sight only, not a link budget. No relay satellites are modeled.
    • Crater-center sites from the gazetteer lie in permanently shadowed floors in the terrain model; they are reference points, not landing recommendations.
    • Not an official NASA product and not a mission planning tool.

    Data & sources

    • JPL DE421 planetary ephemeris — Folkner, W. M., Williams, J. G., Boggs, D. H. (2008), The Planetary and Lunar Ephemeris DE421, JPL IOM 343R-08-003. Kernel: NAIF generic_kernels/spk/planets/de421.bsp.
    • NAIF SPICE lunar frame kernels — moon_080317.tf, moon_pa_de421_1900-2050.bpc, pck00008.tpc from NASA NAIF generic kernels.
    • LRO LOLA terrain — Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter gridded data record LDEM_75S_240M (data set LRO-L-LOLA-4-GDR-V1.0, product version 2.0, 2017), 240 m/pixel south polar stereographic, from the NASA PDS Geosciences Node. Smith, D. E. et al. (2010), The Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter investigation on the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter mission, Space Sci. Rev. 150, 209–241. NASA data, public domain.
    • Sites — Connecting Ridge and Peak Near Shackleton proposed points: Gracy & Lee, 55th LPSC (2024), abstract 1695 (inside NASA Artemis III candidate regions). Shackleton, Haworth and Faustini crater centers: USGS/IAU Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature.
    • Illumination literature — Mazarico, E. et al. (2011), Illumination conditions of the lunar polar regions using LOLA topography, Icarus 211, 1066–1081.
    • Software — Skyfield, NumPy, Pillow. The page itself is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no external services.

    Precomputed data: sites.json · grid.json. Regenerate with python scripts/precompute.py --validate (see README).
